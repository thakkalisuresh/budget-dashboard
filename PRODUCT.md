# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Broader audience — primarily individuals who want to take their personal finances seriously, but the tool should be self-explanatory to anyone who lands on it. Users range from detail-obsessed budget trackers who know every row to occasional check-in users (partners, household members) who just need clarity at a glance. The primary job: understand where money went this month and whether the plan is holding.

## Product Purpose

Fundient is a personal budget dashboard backed by Google Sheets. It turns a live spreadsheet into a real financial tool — tracking monthly spending by category, comparing actual vs. budget, surfacing insights, tracking which card paid for each transaction (with rewards analytics and best-card recommendations), and supporting bank reconciliation. Success looks like: a user opens it, immediately sees their financial position, and knows exactly what to do next (or feels reassured that nothing needs doing).

## Brand Personality

Bold, legible, honest. Fundient feels like a tool built by someone who actually cares about their money — not a bank trying to look friendly, and not a startup trying to look fun. Three words: **precise, confident, considered**.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS blue/indigo dashboards (Tableau, old Salesforce, every fintech that defaults to navy + gold)
- Crypto/neon dark-mode aesthetics — flashy, untrustworthy
- Banking beige — conservative to the point of forgettable
- Startup pastel-and-rounded — consumer toy energy, not serious tool
- Second-order trap: "bold fintech that skips navy" → dark green (Robinhood) or dark purple. Avoid both.

## Design Principles

1. **Data earns the spotlight** — numbers are the hero. UI chrome recedes when data is present; never let decoration compete with a figure.
2. **Bold moments, restrained data** — visual strength belongs in headers, states, and navigation. Charts and tables are clean, precise, and unstyled enough to be readable at a glance.
3. **Confident at first glance** — a new user should immediately understand what this is and feel it's worth trusting. No jargon, no assumed financial literacy.
4. **Earned boldness** — the design can be striking, but every strong choice must serve legibility or hierarchy. Nothing bold for boldness's sake.
5. **One source of truth** — each screen answers one question clearly. No information overload, no competing focal points.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Dark mode is fully supported and must be held to the same visual standards as light. All interactive elements require visible focus states. Financial figures must remain legible at all sizes; avoid relying on color alone to communicate status (over/under budget, etc.).
